Free and open source · MIT

AI writes with total confidence. Sometimes it makes things up.

Assay reads what the AI wrote and tells you which citations and sources are not real, which quotes are not in the source, and which numbers or statistics nobody gave it or got wrong. It works on research, reports, news and company figures alike. You decide what to publish.

Try it nowAdd it to your app

The checks that use your own text run in your browser and send nothing anywhere.

Fifteen checks, each for one job

They answer different questions, so each is its own check with its own import. Use the one that fits; they are not meant to run all at once. Each picture loops to show what the check does.

Citations

Is this paper real?

Assay looks each citation up in public records of papers and books, and says found, look again, or not found. It also checks that the link points to that paper.

Looks it up online
Quotes

Is this quote really in the source?

It searches your source text for the exact sentence. It forgives curly quotes, dashes and capital letters, never different words.

Stays on your device
Figures

Did I make up a number?

It finds every number in the draft and checks that you supplied the same number in your own materials. “three” matches 3.

Stays on your device
Names

Did it invent a partner or an employer?

It reads every name and acronym in the draft and checks that each word of it is in your own materials. It also checks links, DOIs and email addresses.

Stays on your device
Claims

Does it claim more than you said?

“Led a large team” has no number and no name. Assay checks that your materials use a word like “led”, and the word “large”, and says which they never do.

Stays on your device
References

Did it lean on a source you never gave?

It reads author-and-year citations, numbered references, “according to…” and “studies show”, and checks each against your materials. A source nobody supplied is one to check or cut.

Stays on your device
Own materials

Is this one of my own papers?

Put your CV, profile and letters ahead of the public indexes, and a citation they list is confirmed from them. No request is made, and your own work is never sent out to be checked.

Stays on your device
Draft

All of the above, at once.

One call holds a draft to figures, names, references and claims, and lists everything your materials do not back. A citation that trips several checks is reported once.

Stays on your device
Gate

Is this claim sourced well enough to say?

Sources are ranked from your own data down to news aggregators. Some claims, like a loss estimate, need a specialist source. With only press coverage behind it, the gate drops the claim.

Stays on your device
Grounding

Does every number trace to a fact I approved?

Each line carries a tag naming the approved fact it came from. Assay checks that the fact exists and that the line states the same figure.

Stays on your device
Values

Is this figure right for that year?

It asks the source’s own data, such as the World Bank, the IMF, Eurostat or Our World in Data, or figures read off a central bank’s bulletin, for that exact year and unit. A nearby year never counts as a match.

Looks it up online
Links

Does this link work, and is it safe to open?

From a server only. It checks the page is there, and refuses addresses that point into your own network without making any request.

Makes web requests, server only
Support

Does the source actually say it?

A model you supply proposes a sentence from the source as evidence. Assay finds that exact sentence in the source, or marks the evidence as invented. Even when found, a person confirms the reading.

Stays on your device (your model may not)
Retractions

Has this paper been retracted?

It asks Crossref, which includes the Retraction Watch database, whether the paper has a retraction, withdrawal, correction or expression-of-concern notice.

Looks it up online
Legal

Is this case citation real, and is it that case?

It asks CourtListener what case is held under each citation, and catches a real citation attached to the wrong case. Server side only; needs a free token.

Sends the text to CourtListener, server only

Where AI goes wrong

The last seven are real runs: Assay checked the text described, when this page was built. Open “Show the code” to see the call.

A paper that does not exist

Asked for sources, AI models write convincing ones: a real author with a title they never wrote, or a link that goes to a different paper. In a test of free models, some citations were invented outright.

What Assay does

It looks each citation up in public records of papers and books, and says found, look again, or not found. If a record service is down, it says it could not look. It never calls that “not found”.

A quote nobody wrote

You asked for an exact quote from a job posting. The AI wrote “Relocation is available for all staff.” The posting says relocation is for senior staff only.

Assay says

The quote is not in the source text. It may be paraphrased or invented.

Show the code
const posting = "We offer visa sponsorship for this role. Relocation support is available for senior staff only.";
checkQuote(posting, 'Relocation is available for all staff');

An employer nobody had

You gave the AI a CV. Its draft says you worked with the Greenfield Foundation and learned to scale at Acme Capital. Neither name is anywhere in the CV, and neither has a number, so a figures check passes them.

Assay says

Greenfield Foundation: This name is not in your materials. Check it exists and is yours to claim. Acme Capital: This name is not in your materials. Check it exists and is yours to claim.

Show the code
const draft = "We have worked with the Greenfield Foundation, and our earlier role at Acme Capital taught us to scale.";
const materials = "Ada Example founded a payments company in Lagos and managed a team of five at Oneremit Payments.";

A bigger claim than you made

Your CV says you managed a team of five. The draft says you led a large team. “Led” is backed by “managed”, and “large” is a word your CV never uses.

"large": This word makes the claim bigger, and your materials never use it.

A study from nowhere

The draft cites “Smith and Jones (2020)” and says “research shows” your point. Your materials name no such study, and nothing is in the bracketed form the citations check reads.

Assay says

Smith and Jones, 2020: A citation your materials do not contain. If it is real, add it to your materials; if you cannot find it, cut it. research shows: Appeals to unnamed research or experts. Nothing in your materials says this; cut it or give the source.

Show the code
const draft = "As the Smith and Jones (2020) study shows, research shows that informal traders drive growth.";
const materials = "We built a payments app for informal traders.";
checkReferences(draft, [materials]);

Take part: three lines in your app

import { createAssay } from '@extrafemi-ai/assay';
import { createTally } from '@extrafemi-ai/assay/tally';
import { startSharing } from '@extrafemi-ai/assay/share';

const tally = createTally();
const assay = createAssay({ tally });   // counts what it finds, in memory
startSharing(tally);                    // sends the numbers, never text, every ten minutes

What is sent: for each check, how many items were checked and how many came back ok, to check or failed. No text, names or identifiers. The counter keeps no address: it hashes the sender with the day's date, in memory, only to limit what one sender can add in a day, and forgets it. Its code is in the repository, in worker/.

Also found while building Assay, and not counted above because they were not checks: one real bug in Assay itself (a correctly cited paper looked wrong because its title carried markup, fixed in 0.4.0) and ten places where its own text parsing could be made to run for minutes on hostile input. See the changelog and the security notes.

Why a red mark is worth believing

It says what it compared against

Every result names the thing it checked the text with: a public record, your own source text, a list of facts you approved.

It does not accuse when it could not look

Free record services get busy or break. When that happens Assay says “could not check”, and tries again later. It keeps earlier answers and uses them, marked as old, until the service is back.

It is honest about its limits

It can show that a source exists, not that the source says what the sentence claims. Books and reports are thinly covered, so a real work can come back as not found. Read the limits.

Add it to your app in a minute

For developers. It is a small package with no dependencies that runs on Node 20 or later, in browsers and on Cloudflare Workers.

npm install @extrafemi-ai/assay
